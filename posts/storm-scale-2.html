Storming the Scale, Part 2

Last time, we discussed where on the storm scale various mechanics sat. Today we will continue this, looking at sets from 2025.

Here's a reminder of what the scale means.

Rating Meaning
1 Evergreen mechanic
2 Deciduous mechanic
3 Will most likely do again
4 Will likely do again, though there are problems
5 Will do again in the right place
6 May do again in the right place
7 Unlikely, unless the perfect set up exists
8 Very unlikely, unless the perfect set up exists
9 Not likely
10 No

I will also discuss the popularity of mechanics, though this is slightly skewed by my perception and the small playerbase.

Popularity Rating
Very popular People have expressed excitement about the mechanic, and cards that use the mechanic.
Somewhat popular People have showed interest in using the mechanic, or cards that use the mechanic.
Average Although the mechanic isn't exciting, it is generally useful.
Not popular Nobody liked the mechanic, and cards don't see much play.

The Azure Archives

Aether Mana

Popularity: Somewhat popular

As a mechanic designed to allow powerful splashy spells into the game, the concept of aether was well received, however, it hasn't seen much uptake, due to the current scarcity of aether mana sources and its difficulty to include in commander decks.

Power Level: Average

Currently, although individual cards are strong, the difficulty to cast them keeps the mechanic at bay. The overall power level will increase over time as more aether sources (particularly lands) are printed.

Complexity: Low

As the opposite of colorless mana, it was easily understood by players, and tends only to add complexity in deck building.

Storm Rating: 4


Doublecast

Popularity: Very popular

Casting spells in Magic is fun, so being able to copy your spells is (presumably) twice as fun.

Power Level: High

Even with the copies being weaker in most cases, their versatility often outweighs this drawback.

Complexity: Average

Some complexity arises when effects alter the potency of spells, which may require remembering values as the stack resolves. Additionally, since the copy resolves first, this can have some unintended effects.

Storm Rating: 5


Illusory

Popularity: Average

Neither particularly liked or disliked, largely due to its use as a mechanical tool, rather than a splashy build around mechanic. Future uses could see cards turn the mechanic’s weaknesses into strengths, which would likely interest more players.

Power Level: Low

As an actively negative talent keyword, the power of the mechanic itself is low, though this allows for the power level of the rest of the card to increase.

Complexity: Low

Although a triggered ability, most often, illusory triggers due to an opponent targeting the creature with a kill spell, so the result doesn’t change.

Storm Rating: 4

Likely a mechanic that will be seen whenever we visit a set that uses illusions or illusory magic.


Incite

Popularity: Very popular

Often used as a way to simulate temporary card draw, the mechanic was well received by players, as drawing more cards is a powerful tool.

Power Level: Average

Due to its similarity to card draw, the effect is similarly costed, leading to a well-balanced mechanic.

Complexity: High

Although counters are used to track how long a card is incited for, which reduces some memory issues, it adds new complexity by requiring players to remember to take the counters off at the end of each turn.

Storm Rating: 2

Although deciduous, there are times where a set may need to use simpler impulse draw effects to prevent confusion, such as if another mechanic includes cards in exile with counters on them.


Stasis

Popularity: Not popular

A mechanic that doesn't let you use your cards doesn't sit well with players.

Power Level: Low

Many cards with stasis had effects which got stronger with more time counters, but the power usually scaled too slowly for it to be worth keeping the permanent tapped - particularly since you couldn't use the effect the turns you don't untap it.

Complexity: High

Most cards don't interact with the untap step, and therefore remembering to not only not untap a permanent, but also to put a counter on it proved to be quite unintuitive.

Storm Rating: 9


Naturia

Asterisms

Popularity: Very popular

Introduced to represent Naturia's static night sky and offering an alternative aura-like permanent that can come into play before the creature, asterisms have been well received by players.

Power Level: Average

Although some individual designs have proved to be very powerful or problematic, overall the mechanic is kept in check by how difficult it can be to commit to the board.

Complexity: High

The mechanic does present some board complexity, and a large amount of comprehension complexity, and creature asterisms in particular pose some design challenges to avoid players easily setting up a board state where two asterisms are protecting each other.

Storm Rating: 3


Chaos

Popularity: Very popular

As a batching word, causing chaos has enabled designs that make use of unpredictable elements of the game, regardless of the result. This is a very useful tool for adding consistency to what is otherwise an inherently random bag of mechanics, not only increasing interest in the chaos mechanic, but also those mechanics it calls out.

Power Level: Average

Most designs that care about when you cause chaos give small incremental value, which acts as a pay-off for sinking resources into otherwise risky mechanics.

Complexity: Average

Working out what does and what doesn't cause chaos has proven to be somewhat difficult, particularly the difference between a card saying 'random' and 'at random' (the former doesn't cause chaos, and exclusively appears in reminder text).

Storm Rating: 4


Fortune

Popularity: Average

Introduced as a way of causing chaos, fortune mystical spells are highly unpredictable at what they'll do, often not doing what you want with a bad roll. Players didn't care for many of the designs due to this, so the mechanic saw little play.

Power Level: Average

Many designs ended up having a certain chance of doing the thing you wanted them to, and any other roll caused the card to essentially do nothing, despite their costs being about the same as another card that did the same thing.

Complexity: Average

It was important to remember to roll the die as you cast the spell, rather than when it resolved.

Storm Rating: 6


Variance

Popularity: Somewhat popular

Another way of causing chaos, it was much easier to understand the result of your die roll - a lower number resulted in more value from the saga. This led the mechanic to be more popular than fortune.

Power Level: Average

Once again, the power of an individual card largely depended on the result of the die, though many sagas had a 7th chapter so you'd always get some value out of it, followed by a finishing chapter.

Complexity: Average

Most of the complexity came from the timing of when you roll the die.

Storm Rating: 6


Vlad's Castle

Conditions

Popularity: Very popular

As a novel way to mess with the opponent's hand or deck, condition cards have seen a decent amount of interest.

Power Level: Average

The use of conditions tends to favor slower midrange or control strategies, so don't tend to end games on their own.

Complexity: Average

The cards themselves are fairly simple, though as a relatively new card type, have a small learning curve.

Storm Rating: 2 (3 for Wounds specifically)


Resurrect

Popularity: Average

Resurrect hasn't seen much uptake, largely due to the lack of graveyard-based decks that have emerged.

Power Level: High

Thanks to the fairly cheap resurrect costs on cards with the mechanic, being able to play creatures out of your graveyard, either from them dying or from being milled grants quite a significant card advantage.

Complexity: Average

The mechanic has some comprehension complexity, and to use it requires a player to remember to check their graveyard.

Storm Rating: 5


Talent Keywords

Popularity: Very popular

The open ended nature of moving talent keywords around leads this mechanic to be well supported by set releases and offers an interesting puzzle for players to attempt to solve.

Power Level: Average

Cards that care about talent keywords are only as strong as the talent keywords they can utilise.

Complexity: High

Determining what is considered a talent keyword can be a complicated matter that often comes down to checking the list of talent keywords.

Storm Rating: 2


World Enchantments

Popularity: Somewhat popular

The mechanic's popularity largely comes down to whether individual world enchantments are popular - certain effects that are useful in specific popular decks see a decent amount of play.

Power Level: Low

World enchantments are inherently easy to remove by the opponent playing their own world enchantment.

Complexity: Average

Playing a world enchantment requires remembering to remove the opponent's world enchantment, which sometimes gets missed.

Storm Rating: 5


Murders at the Swingin' Speakeasy

Alibi

Popularity: Not popular

Although technically a modal mechanic, the ability to use the face down permanent to draw a card was much less clear than other mechanics that do a similar thing (like cycling), and flipping the card face up had some rather complicated rules interactions.

Power Level: Low

The restriction that alibi had to target a creature to get an effect, although flavorful, led most alibi effects to be quite weak.

Complexity: High

The comprehension complexity for the mechanic is very high, which made working out how to play a card quite difficult.

Storm Rating: 9


Dogan: The Rose and the Tower

Restore

Popularity: Very popular

Restore is a very versatile mechanic, allowing individual cards to have a home in a lot of different archetypes.

Power Level: Low

In general, the requirement to both play the card and then pay the restore cost means cards with this mechanic are quite slow. Restore costs being sorcery speed also limits what kind of decks want to play them.

Complexity: High

The cards have a high comprehension complexity due to the hidden information on their back faces - players need to remember five different cards.

Storm Rating: 5


Festiville

Bind Counters

Popularity: Somewhat popular

Bind counters are a design tool, rather than a splashy mechanic, so tend not to be build-around mechanics. As an alternative method for removing important effects from the opponent's board, they have proven to be useful for midrange strategies.

Power Level: Average

Removal is usually very powerful, but since bind counters have no effect on the power or toughness of most creatures, their usefulness has a limit.

Complexity: Low

As a mechanic that removes effects, rather than adding them, they can usually clean up some board complexity.

Storm Rating: 3


Deck of Many Things

Popularity: Very popular

As a high-risk, high-reward mechanic, D.O.M.T. cards have proved popular as spike-y cards, particularly in small quantities.

Power Level: High

Due to their very high risk, the cards have to be designed to be naturally more powerful than other cards that share their design space.

Complexity: Average

The mechanic has some complexity due the order in which things need to happen when you cast a D.O.M.T. spell being quite specific.

Storm Rating: 9 (for mechanics in a similar design space: 6)

Although popular and fun, the theming of the mechanic means that the available designs were exhausted in Festiville, so it's unlikely the mechanic will return in this form.


Prehistoric

Popularity: Average

Making vanilla creatures (creatures without abilities) matter is novel, though outside of certain token strategies, has a limit to how exciting it looks to players, as vanilla creatures themselves aren't that exciting.

Power Level: Low

Many prehistoric effects weren't powerful enough to act as a pay-off for the inherent disadvantage of playing creatures with no abilities.

Complexity: Low

Playing with creatures with no abilities keeps board complexity quite low.

Storm Rating: 5


Tenacity

Popularity: Average

Tenacity hasn't seen much uptake, partly due to the recency of the set, but also it was dwarfed by other mechanics from the Festiville set.

Power Level: High

Having a creature come back for free after dying is usually a very powerful mechanic.

Complexity: Average

The timing of when a tenacious creature comes back can cause a memory issue.

Storm Rating: 4
